# Benevolence

---

An [NPC](../rule/general/npc.md) with the [benevolence](benevolence.md) motivation believes in sharing what they have with others. However, an [NPC](../rule/general/npc.md) involved in a negotiation must be limited in their benevolence, so that they don't just give the heroes what they need.

Sometimes an [NPC](../rule/general/npc.md)'s benevolence might extend only to a specific group of people, so that a benevolent pirate captain might share their plunder freely with the rest of their crew—but they're still plundering! Other times, an [NPC](../rule/general/npc.md)'s charity might be limited by the fact that they don't have much to give. A benevolent [NPC](../rule/general/npc.md) might be hesitant to give the heroes help because they believe their limited resources are more necessary or could do more good somewhere else.

An [NPC](../rule/general/npc.md) with the [benevolence](benevolence.md) pitfall has a cynical view of the world, believing that no creature has a right to anything just by being alive. The idea of helping others because it's the right thing to do is a preposterous, immature, or inexperienced idea to be laughed off or snuffed out.

Arguments that appeal to a [benevolence](benevolence.md) motivation contend that if the [NPC](../rule/general/npc.md) strikes a deal with the heroes, the people the [NPC](../rule/general/npc.md) cares about will benefit from the deal. Example arguments include the following:

- "If you lend us the Sword of Agathor, we can make Capital safer for your guild by using it to lay your enemies low."
- "If you can [teleport](../movement/teleport.md) us into the dragon's cave, we'll give you half the wyrm's hoard once we cut off the creature's head. That could benefit generations of students at your academy!"
