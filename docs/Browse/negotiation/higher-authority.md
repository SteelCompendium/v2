# Higher Authority

---

An [NPC](../rule/general/npc.md) with the [higher authority](higher-authority.md) motivation remains staunchly loyal to a person or force they perceive as more important than themself. This higher authority could be an organization, a deity or being of great power, a formal leader such as a noble or monarch, a mystical presence or force the [NPC](../rule/general/npc.md) might not fully understand, or a person the [NPC](../rule/general/npc.md) sees as an informal authority figure (an older sibling, a personal hero, and so forth).

An [NPC](../rule/general/npc.md) with the [higher authority](higher-authority.md) pitfall scoffs at the idea of serving another. The [NPC](../rule/general/npc.md) might not believe that all people should be free, but they certainly believe that they personally shouldn't have to answer to anyone.

Arguments that appeal to a higher authority motivation contend that it's in the interest of the higher authority for the [NPC](../rule/general/npc.md) to strike a deal with the heroes. A hero might even tell the [NPC](../rule/general/npc.md) that if the higher authority were in the [NPC](../rule/general/npc.md)'s position, they would take the deal. Example arguments include the following:

- "All great creations honor your god, Malus. If you teach me to forge the Hammer of Azdul, that will be a great honor to bestow upon your god."
- "You know what Jarith the Bold would do? He'd guide us through the vast wasteland of the desert to reach the tower. Will you be our Jarith?"
